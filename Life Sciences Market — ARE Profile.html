Who They Are
Alexandria pioneered the life science real estate niche in 1994. They are the only S&P 500 REIT focused exclusively on laboratory and life science space, owning, operating, and developing what they call Megacampus™ ecosystems — large, collaborative science districts that house biopharma companies, research institutions, government agencies, and venture capital firms in close proximity.
Their model is built on the thesis that clustering the world's leading life science companies in purpose-built campuses drives tenant success — and therefore drives Alexandria's long-term value.

Development Pipeline — The Shift You Need to KnowARE's pipeline is contracting. After years of aggressive development, the Q4 2025 earnings call signaled a deliberate reduction in ground-up starts and CapEx. The $6.35B in active development continues — but the pipeline beyond 2026 is being aggressively pruned. For AEC firms, this means fewer new project RFPs in the near term, and a shift toward TI, campus refresh, and asset repositioning work.
⚠ Q4 2025 Pipeline Contraction — AEC Impact
What changed: ARE is auditing 4 major projects under construction to determine whether to pause, sell, or proceed. CapEx already cut by $300M+ by exiting specific redevelopment projects. Expect fewer new ground-up RFPs through 2026.
Where opportunity shifts: Tenant improvements (TIs), exterior campus refreshes for re-leasing, residential conversion of disposed land parcels, and "activation" of land-banked sites. The BMS Campus Point expansion and Novartis build-to-suit remain mission-critical and proceed.

High-Priority Risks

High Priority
Greater Boston Occupancy Decline
  • Dropped from 94.8% (2024) → 86.4% (2025)
  • 62 properties · $709M in revenue at risk
  • Linked to Kendall Square vacancies · re-leasing pressure intense
High Priority
Life Science Industry Funding Contraction
  • Biotech/pharma unable to raise → may downsize or default on leases
  • 53% revenue from investment-grade tenants provides some protection
  • Preclinical/clinical biotech (10%+ revenue) acutely vulnerable
High Priority
$6.35B Development Pipeline Execution Risk
  • Cost overruns, permitting delays, labor shortages all in play
  • Inability to pre-lease completed space = cash flow risk
  • Non-income-producing assets targeted: 20% → 11–16% of gross assets
High Priority
2026–2027 Lease Expiration Wave
  • $71M expiring 2026 · $72M expiring 2027
  • Expected downtime: 6–24 months per vacancy
  • Stanford Life Science District + One Alexandria Square are key risks
Medium Priority
Seismic and Natural Disaster Exposure
  • Portfolio in seismic zones: SF Bay Area, San Diego, Seattle
  • All-risk insurance: $2.0B per-occurrence limit
  • Wildfire risk (NorCal/PG&E) — additional exposure
Medium Priority
Ground Lease Exposure
  • 31 properties (9%) on ground leases: 19 SF Bay Area · 8 Boston · 2 NYC
  • Default or unfavorable renewal = significant asset loss risk
  • Ground lease expense: 1.6% of NOI in 2025
Medium Priority
Texas Market Underperformance
  • 79.9% occupancy — lowest in the portfolio
  • $28.02/RSF — lowest rent of any market
  • Woodlands campus hasn't achieved Megacampus ecosystem density
Medium Priority
Tenant Concentration — Top 10 = 38.4% of Revenue
  • Top tenant = 6.1% of total revenue — significant concentration
  • M&A, restructuring, or AI-driven lab changes could trigger vacancy
  • Largest single commitment is at Campus Point, San Diego
External Pressure
Biotech Capital Contraction — "Wait-and-See" Leasing
  • VC funds: lowest LP capital raised in a decade
  • Public biotech leasing "very small" in Q4 2025
  • Tenants favoring shorter terms, staying in existing footprints
External Pressure
U.S. Government / NIH Funding Uncertainty
  • US Gov = 9th largest tenant · 1.5% of revenue (NIH/GSA)
  • Budget cuts or continuing resolutions directly reduce demand
  • NIH funding underpins the entire biopharma ecosystem
